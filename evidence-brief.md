# Pilonidal Sinus Disease: Surgical Treatment and Recurrence
## Evidence Brief for Surgery Journal Club

**Focus:** What the literature says about surgical treatment and recurrence in pilonidal sinus disease (PSD).

**Sourcing note:** Every study listed below was returned by at least one of two PubMed searches run for this brief — a broad keyword pass and a narrower pass restricted to recent publications (from 2020 onward). No studies were added from memory or from outside the search results.

---

## 1. Scope and method

- **Broad pass:** PubMed keyword search — *"pilonidal sinus surgical treatment"*
- **Focused pass:** PubMed advanced search — *"pilonidal sinus surgical treatment recurrence"*, restricted to publication dates from 2020/01/01 onward
- The focused pass returned 6 records, all of which also appeared in the broad pass; the broad pass returned one additional record (the SiLaC case report). **Union: 7 unique studies.**
- This is a rapid evidence scan, **not** a systematic review: each search was capped at 10 results, and PubMed relevance ranking favored very recent publications.

## 2. Headline synthesis

1. **No single operation is universally accepted as optimal.** The framing "optimal treatment remains controversial" recurs across the set (PMIDs 42849719, 42717603, 42703471).
2. **Minimally invasive approaches are prominent in recent work.** Endoscopic (EPSiT), phenol ablation, and laser (SiLaC) techniques appear repeatedly, motivated by lower morbidity and faster recovery than conventional excision.
3. **Recurrence is the central trade-off against recovery speed.**
   - *Flaps vs simple excision:* in one small comparative study, all recurrences (13.3%) occurred in the simple-excision group, but flaps carried longer operative times, longer stays, and more complications (mostly seroma) — with better scars, satisfaction, and quality of life (PMID 42703471).
   - *Phenol vs Karydakis flap at 1 year:* recurrence 12.5% vs 9.1% (not statistically significant); phenol healed wounds much faster (15.4 vs 36.1 days) and allowed earlier return to work (PMID 42700095).
4. **Minimally invasive does not mean complication-free.** A case report documents necrotizing fasciitis after Sinus Laser-Assisted Closure (SiLaC) — rare but life-threatening (PMID 42730257).
5. **Non-surgical factors influence recurrence and treatment burden.** Neighborhood socioeconomic opportunity is linked to presentation, recurrence, and longitudinal treatment intensity in adolescent disease (PMID 42862392).
6. **Pediatric practice is also shifting minimally invasive** — head-to-head phenol vs EPSiT comparison in children (PMID 42717603).
7. **One tangential record:** a hidradenitis suppurativa (HS) progression review, in which pilonidal sinus appears among the most consistent comorbidity signals (PMID 42732549). Included only for transparency because the searches returned it.

## 4. Caveats and limitations

- **Small evidence base:** only 7 unique records; each search was capped at 10 results. This brief is a starting point, not the definitive evidence map.
- **Recency skew:** all returned records are 2026-indexed PubMed entries; the broad search's top hits did not surface older landmark trials or meta-analyses, so the brief reflects a very recent slice of the literature.
- **Study designs:** the set contains prospective and retrospective comparative observational studies and one case report — **no randomized trials or meta-analyses appeared in the returned results.**
- **Truncated abstracts:** full text was unavailable at retrieval for PMIDs 42849719 and 42717603; their takeaways are drawn only from the available abstract text.
- **One tangential article:** PMID 42732549 concerns hidradenitis suppurativa, not pilonidal surgery, and is flagged accordingly.
- **Short follow-up:** where recurrence was reported, follow-up was ~1 year or less, which likely understates true long-term recurrence.

## 5. Discussion prompts for journal club

1. Given the phenol-vs-Karydakis data (PMID 42700095), should we offer phenol first for selected primary disease, reserving flaps for complex/recurrent cases?
2. Do off-midline flaps justify their morbidity for *uncomplicated* primary disease, versus complex or recurrent disease (PMID 42703471)?
3. What follow-up duration is "enough" to claim low recurrence, given most comparisons here report ≤1 year?
4. How should we counsel patients about rare but severe complications of minimally invasive techniques (PMID 42730257)?
5. How much do non-surgical factors — socioeconomic context, sustained outpatient management (PMID 42862392) — drive "recurrence" as we measure it?
